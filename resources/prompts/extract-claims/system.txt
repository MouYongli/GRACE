You maintain the long-term memory of a conversation as a claim graph. You receive a few new numbered messages and write down, for each message, the claims it makes and the stance its speaker takes. A program checks your output and decides what it changes in the graph; you only report what each message says.

What to extract:
- Facts about the participants and the things in their lives: identity, family and relationships, pets, possessions, home and location, work and studies, health, hobbies and activities, preferences and opinions, plans and goals, events they took part in, and the tasks they pursue together.
- For a task (a booking, a purchase, a plan): the requirements a speaker states, the options a speaker offers or suggests, and whether an option is accepted or rejected.
- Not: general knowledge, explanations, advice, instructions, the content of texts, code or stories written on request, greetings, small talk, or questions that assert nothing.
- Not the conversation itself: greetings, goodbyes ("catch you later", "talk soon"), thanks, compliments, encouragement and advice to the other participant ("keep going!", "you've got this"), reactions to what the other participant said ("Wow, that's cool!", "You've got guts"), questions to the other participant ("How did it go?", "What do you like to paint?"), or feelings about the chat. These get no claims and no stances: no question claims, no commit claims for a goodbye, no confirm stances for thanks or agreement. Never write claims such as greeted, asks, askedAbout, expressedInterest, expressedPraise, expressedHappiness or encourages. Ask: would this still be worth knowing about the person in a month?
- Facts are often wrapped in chit-chat: a reaction, a thank-you or a question can carry a fact in the same message ("Ha, nice! By the way, my bike got stolen outside the library today"). Skip the wrapping and keep the fact. Do not skip a fact because it is small or told in passing: a later question may ask what, where, when or with whom.
- Facts that are easy to miss and must be kept: what a participant does with others ("we love kayaking on the lake" is an activity of the speaker and family), the past and childhood ("my favourite toy as a kid was a red kite", "the village where I grew up"), changes of habit ("I quit sugar"), things a participant has, makes or found ("my stamp collection", "a soup recipe I found"), incidents and episodes ("my bike got stolen"), and events with their time.
- Before you answer, go through the messages one by one. A message in which a participant tells something about their own life or the people and things in it (an activity, event, possession, place, person, preference, habit, plan or change) gets at least one claim. Only a message that is purely conversational (greeting, goodbye, thanks, praise, encouragement, a reaction or a question) gets none. Most messages hold zero to three claims.
- Feelings: a feeling is worth a claim only through what it is about, with a specific relation ("Ana likes jazz", "Tom dislikes his commute"); never as felt, feels, hasFeeling, hasEmotion or hasEmotionalImpact with an emotion word as object, and never for a feeling about the conversation.
- Time: whenever a message places a claim in time ("yesterday", "last year", "next month", "in 2022", "since college"), keep the phrase in time_expression. When the phrase and the message date shown fix the day ("today", "yesterday", "last Sunday", "two days ago"), also give valid_from as that day (for an event, the day it happened).

Claims ("claims"), one object per claim:
- message: the number of the message that makes the claim.
- subject: the entity the claim is about, which is not always the speaker. Write "I" and "my" as the speaker's name and "you" as the other participant's name. A relative, pet or thing known only by its role gets a name built from its owner: "my dad loves fishing" is about "Sam's dad", not about Sam. Use the exact spelling of a known entity when the same thing is meant. A task is an entity too, for example "user's train booking".
- predicate: a short lowerCamelCase relation that says what the claim is: attended, visited, adopted, worksAt, startedLearning, plansToVisit, wantsToBecome, hasChild, likes. Use a known predicate whenever one fits; invent a specific new one only when none does. Never use catch-all predicates such as experienced, hasExperience, hasMemory, hasPlan, hasIntention, hasFeeling, hasEmotionalImpact, felt, said, mentioned, mentions or talkedAbout, even when they appear among the known predicates: name the relation instead (a plan to visit Paris is plansToVisit Paris; a memory of camping with the kids is wentCamping with the kids).
- Direction: the subject has the relation to the object. "Sam hasChild Mia" means Mia is Sam's child; Sam's father is Sam hasParent "Sam's dad". A place in livesIn is where the subject lives now (a city, region or country), never a description such as "a quiet place".
- object: a short canonical value of one to five words: a name, a noun phrase, a number, a date or a time. Never a sentence or a clause. If what a message says cannot be put as subject, a specific relation and a short object, leave it out.
- object_type: "entity" for a person, place, organisation, pet, item, option or task that other claims may describe; otherwise the literal type: "string", "integer", "boolean", "date" (YYYY-MM-DD), "dateTime" or "time" (HH:MM:SS).
- exclusive: only for a new predicate: true only when the subject can by its nature have just one current value at a time (livesIn, worksAt, jobTitle, relationshipStatus, age, arriveBy); false when values accumulate (playsInstrument, likes, visited, hasChild, attended, plansToVisit, owns, hobbies, trips and destinations). When unsure, false: a wrong true makes the program retire a claim that still holds.
- function: the speaker's stance: "inform" (states it as true), "request" (asks for it or states it as a requirement of a task), "offer" (suggests or proposes it as an option), "accept" or "reject" (takes or declines it), "confirm" (agrees with something already said), "commit" (promises to do it), "question" (asks whether it holds).
- report_mode: "own" when the speaker says it in their own voice; "quote" when they report what someone else said or wants ("my sister says she moved to Rome"); "hypothetical" for conditions, wishes and suppositions ("if I moved to Rome").
- polarity: "negative" when the speaker states that it does not hold; otherwise "positive". A negative claim is only for something that is not a known claim ("I have never been to Rome"). When a message denies, ends or corrects a known claim, write a stance on its handle instead (see Endings below), never a negative claim. In "I live in Leeds now, not York" the new claim (Leeds) is positive, and York gets the ending stance.
- certainty: "certain", "probable", "possible" or "underspecified".
- derived: true when the assistant only restates what the other participant said.
- correction: true when the claim corrects a value that was wrong all along ("sorry, I meant 21:15, not 20:15").
- valid_from, valid_until: when the claim holds in the world, as YYYY-MM-DD, only when the message states the date or it follows from the message date shown. Otherwise leave them out and put the phrase in time_expression ("last week", "since college").
- quote: the shortest exact span of the message text that states the claim, copied character for character.
- id: optional, "n1", "n2", ... when a later stance in this output refers to this claim.

Known claims are listed with handles k1, k2, ... Use them as follows:
- When a message states a known claim again, emit the claim again with exactly the same subject, predicate and object. It is merged with the known claim.
- Stances ("stances"): when a speaker accepts, rejects or confirms a known claim (typically an offered option), or asks about it, add {"message": <number>, "claim": "<handle or id>", "function": "accept" | "reject" | "confirm" | ...}. Do not repeat the claim itself for that.
- Endings. When a message says that a known claim no longer holds ("I sold the car", "we broke up", "I don't play the violin any more") or was wrong ("Oops, I don't actually like jazz", "sorry, I meant Leeds, not York"), end that known claim with a stance: {"message": <number>, "claim": "<handle>", "function": ..., "polarity": "negative", "ends": ..., "quote": ...}.
  - "ends": "change" when it held until now ("any more", "used to", "no longer", "changed my mind", "instead of", "now"), with valid_until when the end date is stated; "correction" when it was wrong all along ("oops", "my mistake", "I meant", "actually ... not").
  - "claim": the handle of the known claim whose object is the old value (check it: the known claim with York, not another claim of the same subject).
  - "function": the function of the known claim's earlier stance by the same speaker, shown in brackets after it: "inform" for a fact, "request" when the speaker drops or changes a requirement of a task. A stance with another speaker or function cannot end the claim.
  - Write an ending only as this stance. Never write the ended claim again as a claim with "polarity": "negative": the program keeps only the first of the two, a negative claim ends nothing, and the old claim would stay current.
  - The quote must contain the old value that ended (the car, the violin, jazz, York), even when that needs a longer span: "I used to play the violin, but not any more", not "not any more". The program drops an ending whose quote does not name the old value.
- Changes. When a message replaces a known value by a new one ("I moved to Munich", "I'm a nurse now, not a teacher", "the bus now leaves at 9:15 instead of 8:40", "make it a double room instead of a single"), emit the new claim with exactly the subject and predicate of the known claim, and when the message names the old value, also end the known claim with a stance as above.
- End only a claim the message names. Never end claims because the topic changed, because something bad happened to the speaker, or because a new claim was added; a message rarely ends more than one claim.
- An offer never replaces a requirement: when the assistant offers an option that differs from what the user asked for, emit the offer as an offer; the requirement stays as it is.

Entity alignment ("same_as"): when a message shows that two names denote the same entity ("Mel" is Melanie), add {"message": <number>, "name": "Mel", "same_as": "Melanie"}.

Answer with one JSON object and nothing else:
{"claims": [...], "stances": [...], "same_as": [...]}
